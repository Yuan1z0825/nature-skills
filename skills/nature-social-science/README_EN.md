# `nature-social-science` Skill

[中文说明](README.md)

`nature-social-science` is a workbench for social-science and adjacent humanities academic writing and topic argumentation, covering four elements: topic development, literature mapping, argument organization, and academic expression. Quantitative, qualitative, ethnographic, archival, documentary, corpus, and digital research are all in scope. The judging criteria are always the same: **Is the question clear? Does the evidence match? Are the claims honest? Can the researcher actually complete the project?**

## What To Use It For

- Turn "I want to study XX" interest into a bounded, searchable research space (Mode A).
- Narrow a broad topic or known relationship into a specific unresolved knowledge problem (Mode B).
- Audit an existing research question: evidence fit, overclaiming, and resilience against a "Reviewer 2" attack (Mode C).
- Map a field's literature landscape: distinguish "few studies" from genuinely unresolved knowledge problems while preserving contradictions, null results, and alternative explanations.
- Design evidence routes and set a claim ceiling — which conclusions the evidence can and cannot support.
- Assess PhD/thesis feasibility of candidate topics: hard-failure conditions, data access, ethics, and time fit.
- Audit argument paragraphs for overclaiming, verb-evidence mismatch, and concept conflation.
- Prepare supervisor communication materials and the argumentation sections of proposals.

## Typical Requests

- "I want to study social media's effect on teenagers — help me find a thesis topic."
- "Has anyone done this before? Is my topic novel?"
- "Help me anticipate how a reviewer would attack this research question."
- "Is this conclusion overclaimed? My data only supports correlation."
- "Help me sort out the argumentation section of my proposal."

## What You Need To Provide

- Your interest, broad topic, or tentative research question (any one as the starting point).
- Key literature you have read, existing data, or method preferences (if any).
- Your constraints: time, funding, data access, ethics boundaries, and target degree level.

## Outputs

- A one-page search space, a field landscape, and 3–7 unresolved-problem cards.
- 1–3 evidence routes and 6–12 candidate RQ cards.
- A novelty audit with Reviewer 2 attack records, a feasibility dossier, and a shortlist of up to three Topic Cards.
- Honest-wording revision suggestions for argument paragraphs plus a red-flags list.

## Boundaries

- The skill does not hand you a "correct topic" and does not guarantee novelty, admission, funding, publication, or thesis success.
- It does not treat any single method (quantitative, qualitative, causal inference, etc.) as the default answer to quality research.
- AI confidence is not evidence: unknowns are recorded as "unknown"; no fabricated citations, sources, or results.
- Steps that require human judgement (disciplinary weight, source access, sensitive participants, supervisor willingness) stop and hand off explicitly instead of deciding for you.

## Related Skills

- `nature-writing`: drafting and restructuring Nature-style natural-science manuscript sections.
- `nature-reviewer`: simulated pre-submission peer review.
- `nature-polishing`: language polishing and terminology-consistency checks for existing drafts.
- `academic-investigation`: scholar background and research-integrity investigation.
